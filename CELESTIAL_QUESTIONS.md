# Twenty-Three Celestial Questions

Questions the Big Pool Bangs framework makes decidable, one sentence each, with what decides it and where it stands.
Statuses: **answered no**, **answered**, **hint**, **consistent**, **open (observational)**, **open (theoretical)**.
Section and label references are to `big-pool-bangs.md` (current release). Updated with each release.

## I. Where are we? — H5 (Our Position – Where Is Nemo), H8 (Precious Rings – Dante's Purgatory)

1. **The Holy Grail.** Is the cold core of our own bang inside our horizon: a naked mass of 10^13 suns or more with no galaxy, no gas and no light? *Decided by:* weak-lensing peaks without counterparts, Einstein rings around nothing, flows converging on empty space (Sec. 3.1). *Status:* answered no in the DES Y3 footprint (11% of sky; no naked mass above ~10^14 suns at z~0.1; `core_search.py`); open elsewhere, Euclid next. Prior odds ~1%, so the null is free.
2. Are we displaced from the centre of our own bang? *Decided by:* void-abundance dipole (2M++, DESI/BOSS voids), P4 (Off-Centre Flow). *Status:* hint (1.5–2σ toward the southern void excess).
3. Is the primordial temperature, and the dark-to-baryon ratio, flat across the observable volume? *Decided by:* SZ temperature–redshift relation, cluster baryon fractions, P12 (Kernel Gradient). *Status:* flat to a few percent; consistent.
4. Does the expansion anisotropy persist with depth along one axis? *Decided by:* CosmicFlows-4 bulk flow, X-ray cluster H0 dipole, supernova dipoles (Sec. 7). *Status:* hint (2σ, direction (260°–305°, −30°)).
5. Does the low-multipole sky carry a neighbour's potential tail? *Decided by:* pre-registered template correlation against 1000 Gaussian skies, P9 (Low-ℓ Tail). *Status:* open (observational).

## II. Where is the second Big Bang? — H2 (Vortex – The Cold Eye of Sauron), H3 (Neighbour – Big Pool Bang Multiverse 2), H4 (Population – The Dark Comic Background), H6 (Neighbour Location), H7 (Pool Model)

6. Is the Cold Spot a compact external source? *Decided by:* a polarization ring at the spot, P1 (Polarization Ring). *Status:* open (observational).
7. Is there a curl in the lensing deflection field? *Decided by:* curl-mode reconstruction at calibrated sensitivity, P8 (Curl Test). *Status:* answered no at current sensitivity.
8. Are the eight extreme spots eight neighbouring bangs? *Decided by:* Johnson–Mehl Monte Carlo of wall placement (Sec. 11f). *Status:* answered no (eight walls in the imprint band in no realization).
9. Do the spots age? *Decided by:* a size–amplitude track in the spot population against Gaussian mocks (Appendix A, P6 (Spot Population)). *Status:* answered no.
10. Is the Axis of Evil a frozen vortex? *Decided by:* cyclonic B-mode pattern aligned with it, P2 (Cyclonic B-modes). *Status:* open (observational; CMB-S4, LiteBIRD).
11. Is there a population of neighbour imprints below threshold? *Decided by:* sub-threshold spot statistics and per-candidate polarization, P6 (Spot Population). *Status:* open (observational).
12. Is any single spot a wall rather than a source? *Decided by:* the tessellation: a wall subtends a fifteenth of the sky and imprints a 30°–50° patch (Sec. 11f). *Status:* answered: at most one wall near last scattering, and it is broad, not a spot.

## III. How does the machine work? — H1 (Bounce – The Big Pool Bang), H8 (Precious Rings – Dante's Purgatory)

13. Is the dark matter particle-free and cold? *Decided by:* direct-detection nulls, small-scale structure, P5 (No Particle DM), P11 (Cold Relics). *Status:* consistent.
14. Is there a transition gravitational-wave background? *Decided by:* pulsar timing, LISA, P3 (Transition GW Background). *Status:* open (observational).
15. Does any primordial black hole above 10^12 kg come from the kernel? *Decided by:* sub-solar-mass mergers with a primordial signature, intermediate-mass holes above 10^-5 of the dark matter, P13 (No Primordial Holes). *Status:* none so far; consistent.
16. How does the kernel make its own light? *Decided by:* the force-chain fraction (bounded by the gamma-ray background) or the reshuffle count of relic/anti-relic annihilation; open problem 4(a). *Status:* open (theoretical); three routes rejected on record.
17. Where do the baryons come from? *Decided by:* an operator the framework does not yet contain; open problem 4(b). *Status:* open (theoretical).
18. Why is space flat without inflation? *Decided by:* a curvature scale of the rebound, not yet computed (Sec. 5.2). *Status:* not addressed.
19. Can the tension of space be derived rather than adopted? *Decided by:* a calculation the framework does not yet have (Sec. 6). *Status:* open (theoretical); domain walls rejected (w = −2/3).

## IV. What came before the Big Bangs? — Sec. 2 and open problem 10

20. What was the state before crystallization, and what was its equation of state? It must hold Planck density without Λ-like pressure for 10^20–10^29 Planck times. *Status:* open (theoretical).
21. **Holy Moly Ghost Waves.** What tore the vacuum? A rogue fluctuation, one seed per patch, with a nucleation action of 184–280 and odds of 10^-80 to 10^-120 per Planck 4-volume, unforced by the thin-wall formula; its statistics fix the cell size, the nearest wall just beyond last scattering, our offset from the origin and the count of neighbours. *Status:* the framework's explanation, computed; tested through what the tessellation predicts in groups I and II (questions 2, 5, 8, 12).
22. Is the vacuum still decaying? *Decided by:* our persistence, which bounds a second decay at an action above 560, twice the first move's. *Status:* answered: not within our horizon, at that bound.
23. Was there a contraction before the bounce? *Status:* answered by choice: no; the framework starts from a decaying vacuum, not a previous universe (a road not taken, Sec. 13), and carries the horizon problem openly as the price.

Group IV has no observation in it beyond what runs through the ghost wave; it is where the framework has the least to test and the most to build.

Ten of the twenty-three have answers, seven of them negative (one only over 11% of the sky).
